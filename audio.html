The Tesseract Bomb

There was once a king who desired a weapon that could never be exhausted.

His engineers forged him a Bomb in the shape of a tesseract.

The Bomb was strange. It did not explode once. It exploded forever.

At every instant it burst outward with the force of a thousand suns, yet at the same instant it collapsed inward with equal fury. It consumed itself and recreated itself in the same breath. Its fire raced to the edge of creation while its ashes fell into a point smaller than dust.

The king asked, "When will it finish exploding?"

The engineers answered, "It cannot. Its explosion is fed by its implosion, and its implosion is fed by its explosion."

The king smiled, for he thought he had obtained infinite power.

Years passed.

The Bomb destroyed no cities. It conquered no enemies. It remained suspended in the vault beneath the palace, endlessly becoming and unbecoming itself.

The king grew old.

"Why does it do nothing?" he demanded.

The oldest engineer replied, "Because all its power is spent on itself."

The king descended into the vault to see.

There hung the tesseract, brilliant and terrible. Worlds of flame blossomed from its faces. Oceans of darkness folded into its corners. It was the most violent thing in existence.

And yet not a grain of dust around it was disturbed.

The king watched for a long time.

Then he understood.

A thing may possess infinite power and still accomplish nothing if all its force is consumed by its own contradictions.

The king left the vault in silence.

When he died, the Bomb remained.

Still exploding.

Still imploding.

Still mistaking motion for change.
